How this map is made

Every path near Dulles, added up. Within 50 miles of Dulles and below 15,000 ft. With take-offs off, Arrivals is exactly the live map's traffic; Take-offs shows the climbs the live map leaves out; Both adds them together. It starts when the farm's receiver started logging and keeps growing; the Pi rebuilds it once a day.

Brighter, not broader. The map is a grid of cells about 110 m (360 ft) square. Each aircraft lights every cell its path crosses, once per pass (per hour), however slowly it flies or however many positions it sends. A route flown a thousand times is as thin as one flown once, just brighter.

Weighted by size. A small plane counts 1; an airliner, regional jet or business jet counts 10, so 100 private planes glow like 10 airliners. Size comes from each type's official wake-turbulence class: light (up to about 7 tonnes: private planes, most helicopters) = 1; medium and heavy = 10. An aircraft of unknown type counts 1.

Colours. By size: small planes in orange, airliners and jets in blue, each brighter with more traffic; where both fly the colours add up towards white. Orange and blue are chosen to stay distinct for most kinds of colourblindness. Single colour: everything together, dim purple to bright yellow. Both on a log scale so quiet routes still show next to the busiest; the numbers under the colour bars are the weights. Contrast brings out faint routes.

Wind. Each hour is filed under Dulles's wind at the time: the report nearest the middle of the hour, the direction the wind blows from (as weather reports give it), to 10°. Calm includes variable winds. The ticks around the dial show how many hours each direction has.
